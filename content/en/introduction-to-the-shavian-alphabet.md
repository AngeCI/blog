I actually already know about this alphabet back in December 2023, via [this video](https://youtu.be/D66LrlotvCA) by RobWords. To my surprise, it’s also already get included in Unicode for some time.

# Features
- Consonants come as voiced-unvoiced pairs. Unvoiced consonants are “tall letters”, which extend above the height of other letters. Voiced consonants are “deep letters”, which extend below the baseline. The corresponding voiced and unvoiced consonant letters are the mirror image of each other. Sibilants are short letters like vowels, except /ŋ/ and /h/.

According to [Shavian Alphabet Info](https://shavian.info/alphabet/), the Shavian letter ordering is actually a bit different from the ordering used in Unicode, possibly due to misinterpretation of the letter ordering spec by Unicode.

# Spelling rules
[The “Shavian Alphabet Info” website](https://shavian.info/spelling/) and [Read Lexicon](https://readlex.pythonanywhere.com/) list various established spelling rules for the Shavian alphabet:
- “-es” and “-ed” as plural and past endings change their Shavian spelling according to their actual pronunciation. When there is explicit vowel sound, they have 𐑩 instead of 𐑦. This rule distinguishes some minimal pairs such as “parted” 𐑐𐑸𐑑𐑩𐑛 and “partied” 𐑐𐑸𐑑𐑦𐑛, “roses” 𐑮𐑴𐑟𐑩𐑟 and “Rosie’s” ·𐑮𐑴𐑟𐑦𐑟.
- The “ng” and “nk” sound is always spelled with a 𐑙, never a 𐑯, unless it crosses a synchronic morpheme boundary.
- **Do not double any letters**, unless it crosses a synchronic morpheme boundary.
- One-syllable light words are **always** spelled as if they are **stressed**, with the following exceptions:
- - The indefinite articles “a” and “an” are spelled as “𐑩” and “𐑩𐑯” respectively.
- - “The”, “of”, “and”, “to” and “for” are usually abbreviated into a single letter, i.e. “𐑞”, “𐑝”, “𐑯”, “𐑑” and “𐑓”.
- Insert a 𐑩 before syllabic consonants.
- - This includes contractions as well. “Didn’t, doesn’t, haven’t, hasn’t, wasn’t, wouldn’t, couldn’t, shouldn’t” all end in -𐑩𐑯𐑑.
- The “wh” sound spells as either 𐑢 or 𐑣𐑢, but never 𐑢𐑣.
- The vowel 𐑩 is **always** unstressed. In rare case where it’s stressed in some foreign words, use 𐑻︀ (𐑻 without its 𐑮 part, might not render correctly on some devices).
- Likewise, the letter 𐑼 is **always** unstressed. In cases when it’s stressed, use 𐑻.
- Word final -𐑦 vs -𐑰:
- - Final unstressed “-y” and “-ie” is -𐑦: “trusty” 𐑑𐑮𐑳𐑕𐑑𐑦
- - Final stressed “-ee” is -𐑰: “trustee” 𐑑𐑮𐑳𐑕𐑑𐑰
- Usage of rhotic vowels:
- - Rhotic ligatures can cross syllable boundaries, but not synchronic morpheme boundaries.
- - “Korea” 𐑒𐑼𐑾, “career” 𐑒𐑼𐑽
- The Shavian spelling distinguishes Mary-merry-marry, cot-caught, and father-bother, which are not distinguished in some English dialects/accents.
- Include most optional schwas: “History ”is /hɪstəri/ 𐑣𐑦𐑕𐑑𐑼𐑦 instead of /hɪstri/ 𐑣𐑦𐑕𐑑𐑮𐑦, “dictionary” is /dikʃənəri/ 𐑛𐑦𐑒𐑖𐑩𐑯𐑼𐑦 instead of /dɪkʃənri/ 𐑛𐑦𐑒𐑖𐑩𐑯𐑮𐑦. This rule (or advice?) makes Shavian spelling more accent-neutral.
- Stress is not represented in the Shavian spelling, and must be inferred from phonological restrictions and context.

# Fonts
Windows’ built-in font “Segoe UI Historic” is capable of displaying Shavian letters, though the letters 𐑥 and 𐑯 looks a bit stretched.

# Keyboards
[The “Shavian Alphabet Info” website](https://shavian.info/keyboards/) produces IMEs for Shavian. [An online keyboard](https://shavian.info/type/) is also provided there (though not quite suitable to be used on phones).

Lightingale Community also has a project called [Raven](https://kb.ltgc.cc/raven/), which has Shavian (as well as some other writing systems, though in a less mature state) typing plans for Linux. [Here](https://kb.ltgc.cc/raven/en-alt.html) is the documentation specific to the Shavian alphabet.
